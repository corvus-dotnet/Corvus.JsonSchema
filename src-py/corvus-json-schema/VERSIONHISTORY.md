# Version History

The version history of `corvus-json-schema`, the Python port of the Corvus.Text.Json V5 runtime evaluator. It is versioned independently of the Corvus NuGet packages, whose history is in the repository's [VERSIONHISTORY.md](../../VERSIONHISTORY.md).

## V0.1.1

V0.1.1 makes `pattern`, `patternProperties` and `format: regex` answer as V8 does where version 0.1.0 gave another answer, raised an error or refused the schema, and it bounds what a regular expression may cost. The API has one new option and two new exceptions. A validation that used to run without end, or to take hundreds of megabytes, now raises, and so does the compilation of a schema with a pattern that used to take hundreds of megabytes.

### New features

- **A match of a regular expression has limits of time and of memory, and a pattern has limits of size.** A schema and an instance may both come from outside, and `cjs.compile({"pattern": "^(a+)+$"})("a" * 40 + "!")` did not return in any time a caller would wait, which is all that a denial of service needs. `compile`, `generate_module` and `CompileOptions` take a new option, `pattern_match_limit`: the seconds one match of a `pattern` or `patternProperties` regular expression against one string may take, by the clock. It is `1.0` unless given, and `None` or `0` turns the limits of a match off for a caller who trusts both the schema and the instances. A validation whose match reaches a limit raises the new `PatternMatchLimitError`, from the validator and from `evaluate`, with a results collector or without, so the instance is neither valid nor not valid. A schema with a pattern over a limit of size raises the new `PatternTooLargeError` when it is compiled, which is a `SchemaCompilationError` and is never raised for a pattern that is not valid. The limits of size are 32,768 characters of pattern, 100 groups inside each other, 32,768 characters of the translation for Python's engines, and 4,096 copies of what a count of more than one term repeats in a pattern that the `regex` package runs. The names and the meanings are those of `corvus-json-schema-rs`, and `README.md` lists what is left between the two packages. The command line takes `--pattern-match-limit`, and a generated module carries the limit it was generated with.

  The standard library's `re` cannot be stopped once a match has started, and the `regex` package takes a timeout. So with the limit in force a pattern goes to `re` only when the package proves that `re` matches it in time proportional to the text: at every choice the next character of the text says which way to go, with five bounded exceptions that `README.md` lists. Such a pattern has no limit of time and needs none, and a match costs what it did before. Every other pattern goes to the `regex` package with the limit. Of the 124 patterns of the jsonschema-benchmark corpora that need a regular expression, 117 are proved, and four of the other seven take a time that grows as the square of the text or faster. Of 220,000 random patterns, 33,000 were proved, and `re` matched each of those in time proportional to the text on CPython 3.10, 3.12 and 3.14.

  The limit is of time on the clock. The `regex` package's timeout counts the time of the whole process on the processor, and the package runs a match without the interpreter's lock, so with other threads at work a timeout of 1 second came after 0.24 seconds with three of them and after 0.07 with fifteen. The timeout is used as an allowance of processor time, and the clock decides: a first try of a thirty-second of the limit with nothing measured around it, so a match that answers costs what it did, and then tries that are allowed what should come to the limit on the clock. The error is not raised before nine tenths of the limit have passed, and no try is allowed more than is left of twice the limit, which bounds a match at about twice the limit whatever the other threads do. With three or more other threads at work throughout, a try gets less than the limit on the clock. With a limit of 1 second the error came after 0.95 to 1.12 seconds with up to fifteen other threads at work, and after 1.8 to 1.9 seconds with seven threads that stopped during the match.

  The engine takes a repetition of one class as one step of a match, however much of the text it runs over, and looks at its clock every 256 steps, so its timeout came seconds late for a class of many ranges and a long text: after 6.2 seconds for a property over 100,000 characters, and after 51 to 68 for ten million. A class of more than 24 ranges under a repetition is written so that each character is a step, and a text of more than 32,768 characters is matched by the pattern written so for every such repetition. Before the first match of a pattern the engine also builds the tables of a search for each literal, in a step its timeout does not interrupt, which took 27 seconds for a literal of 8,000 characters that repeats itself, so a long literal is given to it in pieces of 256 characters.

  Both engines keep what a group held each time it closes, until the match ends, and fill hundreds of megabytes in a second: 480 empty groups in a repetition took 740 MB for a text of 100,000 characters and ended in a `MemoryError`, in either engine. A group that no backreference reads now does not capture inside a repetition. For the groups that are read, and for the 35 to 56 bytes that the `regex` package keeps for each iteration of a repetition, a text has a limit of length in a pattern that the `regex` package runs, once the first try at a match has been abandoned: 1,048,576 characters, and fewer for a pattern with groups that are kept for every repetition (262,144 for `^(?:(a)|b)*\1$`, 175 for a repetition inside a lookahead inside a repetition). A longer text raises `PatternMatchLimitError`, which says so and has the number as `most_characters`. The most that one match took at the longest text its pattern lets through is 90 MB.

  A pattern is not refused for how the package writes it. A plain term is as long translated as it was written (`a?` was `(?:a)?`, and a character beyond ASCII was an escape of six characters), a class of a property counts once towards the length of a translation however many times the pattern has it, and a count of one character or class is written for the `regex` package without copies where the copies would be over the limit. Of the 98,839 patterns of the test data, one is refused as too large (it was fifteen), and nine or ten have a text whose match reaches the limit of time, by the version of Python.

- **The properties and the case pairs of Unicode 17, from the package's own tables.** The package had the property names and the case pairs of Unicode 16, and took the characters of a property from the `regex` package, which has the version of Unicode of its release: 16 in 2025.9.1, and 18 in 2026.9.29, which has other characters than Unicode 17 for 89 of the 440 property expressions of ECMA-262. The package now carries the names and the characters of every property and the case pairs of Unicode 17, generated from the tables that every port of the evaluator reads. So `\p{sc=Sidetic}`, `\p{sc=Tolong_Siki}`, `\p{sc=Beria_Erfe}` and `\p{sc=Tai_Yo}` are names (they made a pattern that is not valid with the `u` flag, as they do in V8 13.6, which has Unicode 16), `cjs.compile({"pattern": "(?i:^\ua7ce$)"})("\ua7cf")` is `True`, and the answer for a property is the same with every release of the `regex` package. A property is written as ranges of characters for `re`. For the `regex` package it is written as the package's own property with what is different between its version of Unicode and 17 (the ranges it lacks beside it, or `[^\P{..}ranges]` for the ranges it has too many of), and what is written is checked against the tables for every character. `\p{L}` costs 35 nanoseconds for a character with `regex` 2026.9.29 and 9 with 2025.9.1, where its 684 ranges cost 44 to 950.

### Bug fixes

- **A digit of a pattern.** The translator asked Python whether a character of a pattern is a digit, and Python takes the decimal digits of every script, of the version of Unicode that the interpreter has. So `^(a)\1\u0663$` (a reference and an Arabic-Indic digit) was not valid with the `u` flag, where V8 matches `"aa\u0663"`, `^a{\u0663}$` was a count of three and valid for `format: regex`, where in V8 the braces are literal and the pattern is not valid with the `u` flag, and `^\0\u0663$` was not valid with the `u` flag. A digit of a pattern is now one of `0` to `9`.
- **`\B` in an empty string.** In ECMA-262, `\B` holds wherever `\b` does not, so it holds in an empty string, which has no word character and so no word boundary. The standard library's `re` module had `\B` never match in an empty string before CPython 3.14, and the package handed `\B` to it unchanged. `cjs.compile({"pattern": "\\B"})("")` returned `False`, where the instance is valid, and so did any pattern that reaches a `\B` with an empty string, such as `^\B$` or `^(\d|\B){2}`. A pattern used for `patternProperties` or `propertyNames` was affected for the property name `""`. `\B` is now translated as "not at a word boundary", `(?!\b)`, which is right on every version. `\b` was already right, and a string that is not empty was never affected. A test checks both at every position of every string of up to three characters from a small alphabet, through the standard library's engine and through the `regex` package. Version 0.1.0 is affected on CPython 3.10 to 3.13, and is not on CPython 3.14.
- **Backreferences.** In ECMA-262 a backreference to a group that has not matched matches the empty string, and the groups inside a repetition lose their values at the start of each iteration. The two engines the package uses fail such a reference and keep a value from one iteration to the next, and the standard library's refuses a reference that comes before its group or is inside it. So `cjs.compile({"pattern": "^(?:(a)|b)\\1$"})("b")` and `cjs.compile({"pattern": "^(?:(a)|b)*\\1$"})("ab")` returned `False`, where the instances are valid, and `^\1(a)$` and `^(a\1)$` raised `SchemaCompilationError`, where the patterns are valid. Each reference is now written from where its group is in the pattern. One that can never be read after its group has matched (it is inside the group, before it, or in another alternative) is written as nothing. One whose group has always matched when it is read stays a reference. For any other, the pattern is run by the `regex` package, where an empty group of the same name resets the group at the start of the pattern and of each iteration. On CPython 3.10 a pattern with a reference to a group inside a repetition is also run by `regex`, because the standard library's engine did not put back what such a group held when it backtracked: `^([^a]?a+){2,3}?\1a$` did not match `aaaa` there. A pattern with no backreference is translated as before.
- **Modifier groups.** The modifier groups of ECMAScript 2025, `(?i:...)`, `(?m:...)`, `(?s:...)` and `(?-i:...)`, raised `SchemaCompilationError`. They now have ECMA-262's meaning, written out by the package so that it is the same on every version of Python. Inside `s` the dot matches any character. Inside `m`, `^` and `$` hold at each of the four line terminators (line feed, carriage return, U+2028 and U+2029), where Python's own multiline flag knows the line feed only. Inside `i`, characters are compared by simple case folding, or by the uppercase form of a UTF-16 code unit in a pattern that is valid only without the `u` flag. So `(?i:k)` matches the Kelvin sign (U+212A) and `(?i:\w)` matches the long s (U+017F), and in a pattern without the `u` flag neither does. The case tables are those of Unicode 16, which V8 13.6 has, in `_casefold.py`. `scripts/embed_casefold.py` makes that file from the Unicode Character Database, and its 2,595 sets were checked against V8 over every character. A backreference inside `i` has its own entry below.
- **An iteration that matches nothing.** ECMA-262 refuses an iteration that matches nothing once the minimum count is met, so such an iteration leaves no group set. Both engines accept one. `cjs.compile({"pattern": "^(?:(a?))+\\1b$"})("ab")` returned `True`, where V8 says the instance is not valid, and so did `^(?:(?=(a)))?\1b` for `"ab"`. A repetition whose body can only match nothing is now written as its body once, or as nothing for a minimum of 0, which is what ECMA-262 makes of it. A repetition that holds a group which is read without its having matched is written as its minimum count and then a rest in which every iteration has to take a character.
- **Group names.** Two groups may have one name when they are in separate alternatives (`(?:(?<a>x)|(?<a>y))\k<a>`), and a name may be written with `\u` escapes (`(?<\u0061>a)\k<a>`). Both raised `SchemaCompilationError`. A name that is not an identifier, as in `(?<1a>a)` or `(?<a-b>a)`, was accepted and is now refused, as V8 refuses it.
- **Patterns the `regex` package runs.** Two faults showed only in a pattern that goes to that package (one with a lookbehind or a Unicode property, say). An empty class, `[]`, matched any character there, because the package reads `[^\s\S]`, which the class was written as, in that way: `(?<=a)[]` was valid for `"ab"`. A reference inside a repetition with a maximum count is not seen by the package where it decides what it need not try again: `(?<=)^(?:(?:(a|)|){2})?(?:x\1)?$` was not valid for `"axa"`. In a pattern with a reference, a repetition that holds a reference or a group that is reset is now written without a count, as copies and alternatives, and a reference to an empty group follows every repetition. A pattern is refused with `SchemaCompilationError` when that would take its translation past 65,536 characters, as a count inside a count can: `^(?:(?:(a)|b){300}){300}\1$` is. `format: regex` still says such a pattern is valid.
- **Patterns that raised from inside an engine.** `a{99999999999999999999}` raised `OverflowError` out of `cjs.compile`. A count of 2,147,483,647 or more is now what it is in V8: no maximum when it is one, and a minimum that no text reaches. A minimum that large over a body that can match nothing, as in `(?:a|){99999999999999999999}`, is refused with `SchemaCompilationError`. `(?:(?=.(a.|){3}|[^a]x|\1)(?<=a)|[ax][^a])*?x$` raised `MemoryError` for `"aaax"`. The `regex` package runs another iteration of a repetition for as long as each one changes what a group holds, even when none takes a character. In a pattern for that package, a repetition that holds a group some reference reads now has to take a character in every iteration past its minimum.
- **A backreference inside a case-insensitive group.** ECMA-262 compares the text of the group and the text at the backreference character by character, each by its Canonicalize: simple case folding with the `u` flag, and the uppercase form of a UTF-16 code unit without it. The package handed the comparison to the `regex` package, whose own comparison is another one. So `cjs.compile({"pattern": "^(?i:(.)\\1)$"})("i\u0130")` returned `True`, where V8 says the instance is not valid (U+0130 folds to itself), and in a pattern that is valid only without the `u` flag `^(.)(?i:\1)\&?$` was valid for `"s\u017f"`, `"k\u212a"`, `"\u00df\u1e9e"` and `"\u1f80\u1f88"`, none of which V8 accepts. Neither engine's comparison can be used. Measured for every cased character against every character, the `regex` package (2026.9.29, which has Unicode 17) equates 104 ordered pairs that ECMA-262 does not with the `u` flag (`i` with U+0130, `I` with U+0131, and the 100 pairs that Unicode 17 added, which V8 13.6 does not have) and 752 without it. The 2024.11.6 release of the same package equates 4 and 652. The standard library's `re` takes `i` and `I` for U+0130 and misses between 100 and 234 of ECMA-262's 3,034 pairs, by the version of Python. The comparison is now written out in the pattern, with the tables of `_casefold.py` that the rest of a case-insensitive group uses: a loop takes one character of the text for each character of the group and asks that the two are the same or are in one set of the tables. The `regex` package runs it. A group that can hold no character with another case, such as `(\d+)`, keeps a plain backreference, and `(?i:(\d+)-\1)` is now run by the standard library's engine. The comparison costs a few microseconds for each character compared, where a plain backreference costs a fraction of one. A group that can hold any character has a translation of about 54,000 characters, which takes about 0.2 seconds to compile and holds about 3 MB.
- **An iteration that matches nothing, inside a lookaround.** A lookaround is left by the first way through it, and its groups keep what that way gave them. ECMA-262 refuses an iteration that matches nothing once the minimum count is met, and goes on to the next way through the body. Both engines accept the iteration and leave the repetition there, so another way is first. `cjs.compile({"pattern": "^(?=((?:|a)*))\\1$"})("a")` returned `False`, where the instance is valid: in ECMA-262 the group holds `a`, and the engines left it empty. So did `^(?=(()()())((c{0,}?)?))\5$` for `"c"`, and `(?<=((?:|a)*))b\1` differed in the same way. In a lookaround with a group that is read outside it, every repetition whose body can match nothing is now written as the repetitions that hold such a group already were, with a rest in which each iteration has to take a character, and the pattern is run by the `regex` package. A fuzz of 24,000 such patterns against V8 found the fault in 5 of the first 8,000.
- **A pattern that is valid only without the `u` flag, by code unit.** Without the `u` flag ECMA-262 reads a pattern and matches a text by UTF-16 code unit, and the package did both by character. That showed for a character beyond the Basic Multilingual Plane. `cjs.compile({"pattern": "^\\-?.$"})("\U00010400")` returned `True`, where V8 says the instance is not valid (the dot takes one of its two code units), `^\-?[^a]{2}$` was not valid for it, and in `^\-?\U00010400*$` the quantifier repeated the whole character, where V8 repeats its second surrogate. Such a pattern is now translated from its code units, and a text it is given is matched as its code units: each character beyond the Basic Multilingual Plane as its two surrogates. That is done only for a pattern that can tell the difference, which is one that can take a character from U+D800 up or that has a lookaround or a `\B` (`\B\&?` holds between the two surrogates of `"a\U00010400a"` and nowhere else in it). For any other pattern, `^[\w\-\.]+\&?$` say, nothing is added to a match, and for one that can, a text of ASCII characters is passed as it is. A group name of a character beyond the Basic Multilingual Plane is still one name (`(?<\U00010400>.)\k<\U00010400>`).
- **A translation an engine does not compile.** A pattern whose translation the `regex` package did not compile was reported as a pattern that is not valid. That is the right answer for a property the package does not have, as in `\p{Nope}`, and it would have hidden a fault of the translation as a refusal. Each `\p{...}` is now asked about by itself when the pattern is read, and a translation that the engine then does not compile raises `RuntimeError` with a message that says it is a fault of the package and shows the translation. None of the 79,221 patterns of the test data, and none of the fuzz patterns, has such a translation.
- **A negated class with a property both ways.** `cjs.compile({"pattern": "[^\\p{L}\\P{L}]"})("ab")` returned `True`, where the instance is not valid: the class takes no character. The `regex` package (2025.9.1 to 2026.9.29 at least) reads a class that has a property and the same property negated as every character, and then drops the `^` before it, so `[^\p{L}\P{L}a]` and `[^\p{sc=Greek}\P{Script=Greek}]` took any character too. A negated class with a `\p{...}` and a `\P{...}` is now written as a character that the class without the `^` does not take, which that reading does not touch. Any other class is written as before.
- **The oldest release of the `regex` package.** The package asked for `regex` 2024.11.6 or later, and it now asks for 2025.9.1 or later. Two faults of earlier releases show in what the package writes for it. A `\b` or `\B` in a pattern that the `regex` package runs is written `(?a:\b)`, as the pattern is compiled without the ASCII flag there. Releases before 2025.7.34 do not apply that flag inside a group, so with one of them `cjs.compile({"pattern": "(?<=\u00e9)\\b"})("\u00e9")` returned `True`, where the instance is not valid, as `\u00e9` is not a word character in ECMA-262. With 2024.11.6, 18 of 4,000 generated case-insensitive patterns had a wrong answer for this reason. Release 2025.8.29 does not compile a dot that takes every character where it is an alternative, as in `(?<![^]|\r)$`, and raised `AttributeError` out of `cjs.compile` for 55 of 14,000 generated patterns with modifiers. With 2025.9.1 the package gives the answers it gives with the latest release for every pattern of the test data and for every generated pattern.
- **Property names that only the `regex` package has.** ECMA-262 says which names a `\p{...}` may have: a value of General_Category by itself or after `General_Category=` or `gc=`, a value of Script after `Script=`, `sc=`, `Script_Extensions=` or `scx=`, and one of the binary properties that it lists, each spelled as the Unicode Character Database spells it. The package asked the `regex` package whether it had a name, and that package has many more: a script by itself, `Is` and `In` prefixes, POSIX names, block names, and every name in any case. So `cjs.compile({"pattern": "\\p{Greek}"})` returned `True` for `"\u03b1"` and `False` for `"p{Greek}"`, where V8 says the opposite: `\p{Greek}` is not valid with the `u` flag, and without it `\p` is the letter `p` and the braces are literals. The same held for `\p{IsGreek}`, `\p{InGreek}`, `\p{Latin}`, `\p{alpha}`, `\p{Punct}`, `\p{XDigit}`, `\p{lu}` and `\p{L&}`, and `\p{InF}` raised `OverflowError` out of `cjs.compile`. A name is now looked up in a table that the package carries (`_properties.py`, made from Unicode 16 by `scripts/embed_properties.py`), and each expression is written for the engine with its property named, so the engine is never asked what a name means. `\p{ASCII}` and `\p{Any}` are written as ranges, `\p{Assigned}` as every character that is not unassigned, and `\p{Changes_When_NFKC_Casefolded}` (`\p{CWKCF}`) from ranges in the table: the `regex` package does not have that property, so the package read the pattern without the `u` flag, and `\p{CWKCF}` was not valid for `"A"`. Of 41,133 names that some engine might read (those of the Unicode Character Database in several spellings, the `regex` package's own, and names of Java, PCRE2, .NET and POSIX), 1,690 are ECMA-262's, and the package takes those and no others. With `regex` 2025.9.1, whose tables are Unicode 16 as V8 13.6's are, each of the 433 expressions that V8 takes has the characters it has in V8, over all 1,114,112 code points. `regex` 2026.9.29 has Unicode 17, and 99 of them differ there by the characters that Unicode 17 added or changed.
- **`\k` in a class, in a pattern with a named group.** In a pattern with a named group, ECMA-262 has `\k` only as a reference to one, in the grammar without the `u` flag too. So `(?<n>a)[\k]` is not valid, and `cjs.compile` accepted it with `\k` read as the letter `k`. It now raises `SchemaCompilationError`. In a pattern with no named group, `[\k]` is still the letter, as Annex B says. This came from 8,758 patterns built around the forms of Annex B (`\p`, `\k`, `\u{...}`, `\c`, `\x`, octal escapes, `\8` and `\9`, braces and brackets as literals, and the syntax that other engines have), given to V8: these eight were the only ones the package and V8 disagreed on.
- **A repetition in a lookbehind, where a group has another value on another try.** The `regex` package remembers the places where what follows a repetition has failed and does not try them again, unless it sees a reference there, as the outcome then depends on what a group holds. The package writes a reference to an empty group after each repetition for that. In a lookbehind, which the engine matches backwards, what follows a repetition is at its left, and the reference was written at its right. So `cjs.compile({"pattern": "(.)?(?<=(?=\\1\\b)(?:[^a]{2,})*)\\1xa"})("xxxa")` returned `False`, where V8 says the instance is valid: the lookbehind was tried with the group holding `x`, failed, and was not tried again with the group empty. The reference is now written at the left of a repetition in a lookbehind.
- **Repetitions inside repetitions that can match nothing.** ECMA-262 refuses an iteration that matches nothing once the minimum count is met, and leaves the repetition there. Both engines accept such an iteration and go on to what follows the repetition, and when that fails they leave the repetition as ECMA-262 does and try what follows again. One repetition inside another does that at each level, so a text that does not match took a time that doubles with each level. The answers were right and came late: `(?:\-|)(?:((?:[ax]x\1|\2)?(-|\2{2}){3,}){1,}|(?<n0>(ax){3,4}|\4){0,0}?){3}-$`, a pattern of the test data, took 234 seconds for its 133 texts of six characters or fewer, and 86 seconds for one of them. Where no reference reads a group of it, a repetition only says which texts match, so a repetition whose body can match nothing, with a repetition inside it or around it, is now written with a body that cannot: `^(?:(?:a|)*x?)*$` is written as `^(?:a+x?|x)*$` would be. In a pattern with a reference that reads a group, the same is done for each repetition that has no such reference and no such group inside it and is not inside a lookaround whose groups are read. A repetition whose body matches nothing only where an assertion holds, under a minimum count of 1 or more, is left as it is written, and so is a pattern that would pass the size limit written that way. The pattern above now takes under 0.1 seconds for its texts. Of the 32 patterns of the test data that took more than five seconds for their texts on some run, 22 now take less than one second, and the other ten have a reference that reads a group inside such repetitions.
- **A repetition of nothing but a repetition.** `cjs.compile({"pattern": "(?:" * 20 + "a*" + ")*" * 20 + "b"})`, a pattern of 127 characters, took 6.5 seconds and 331 MB, and with 25 groups it ran out of memory. The package wrote such a pattern as nineteen groups with a `+` inside each other, and the `regex` package compiles each `+` as two copies of its body. Where no reference reads a group, `(?:Y+)+` matches what `Y+` does and `(?:Y+)*` what `Y*` does, so a repetition with no maximum whose body is nothing but another one is now written as the inner one: the pattern above is `(?:a)*b`, in linear time in either engine. Written one inside the other, both engines also tried every way to share a text between the two.

These came from testing the package against the [ECMA-262 regular expression test data](https://github.com/corvus-dotnet/ecma262-regex-test-data), which has V8's answers for 98,839 patterns and their texts in thirteen files, and against patterns that were generated and given to V8. On CPython 3.10, 3.12 and 3.14, with the limit of a match at its default of one second, every answer the package gives for the test data is V8's (4,107,603 texts), and no pattern that is not valid is accepted. Fifteen patterns are refused as too large: one of the `lookarounds` file, whose translation is too long, and fourteen that repeat one character more than 4,096 times in a pattern that is tried at every place in the text, as `\u{10400}` does when it is read without the `u` flag. Ten or eleven patterns, by the version of Python and by what else the machine is doing, have a text whose match reaches the limit, where V8 answers within 10,000 backtracks: all but one are in the `backreferences` file, and each has a reference that reads a group inside repetitions that can match nothing. Before the limit those took from 28 seconds to more than two minutes for their texts. Of 188,803 generated patterns with 44.7 million texts (repetitions inside repetitions, lookaheads and lookbehinds with groups, case-insensitive groups and backreferences), on CPython 3.12, 73 are refused as too large (each repeats the letter `u` 10,400 times or more in a pattern that is tried at every place, as above), 68 have a text whose match reaches the limit, and 191 have an answer that is not V8's. Each of those 191 is one of the first three places where V8 13.6 departs from ECMA-262, and they are the 191 of the run before the limits. Of 106,259 patterns built around property names and the forms of Annex B, with 7.2 million texts, the package differs from V8 13.6 on 333 texts in 16 patterns, each a `\p` or a `\P` of one of the four scripts of Unicode 17, once the three other places where V8 departs from ECMA-262 are set aside. Fifty-four of them repeat a letter more than 4,096 times in a pattern that is tried at every place, and are refused as too large. An adversarial probe of 183 patterns of at most 1,000 characters against texts of at most 100,000 (nested repetitions, backreferences in loops, nested counts, deep nesting, long alternations, lookbehinds, case-insensitive backreferences and large property classes) ends, on each of the three versions, in 100 answers, 45 errors of the limit of a match (each after 1.00 to 1.02 seconds on the processor), 37 refusals for size and one pattern that is not valid. Before the limits 59 to 69 of them gave no answer in 8 to 20 seconds and 8 to 15 ended in `RecursionError`, and no answer that came then is another answer now. The longest compilation in the probe is 0.29 seconds and the most kept for a pattern 2.7 MB. The most memory a process took is 488 MB, during a match that reached the limit (110 groups of one name in a repetition against 100,000 characters): the `regex` package has no limit on the memory of a match, which is bounded by its time. `README.md` says what is written out so that it is exact, what the limits are and which engine runs a pattern, and where V8 itself departs from ECMA-262.

## V0.1.0

The first release of the Python port of `Corvus.Text.Json.RuntimeEvaluator`, by way of its TypeScript port.

### New features

- **A JSON Schema evaluator for Python.** It covers draft 4, 6, 7, 2019-09 and 2020-12 and passes all 7,967 tests of the JSON-Schema-Test-Suite, including `draft4/optional/zeroTerminatedFloats.json`, which the C# and TypeScript runners exclude. A schema is compiled once into specialised Python, one function per schema node with leaf schemas tested inline, that validates the values `json.loads` produces. It is pure Python, with the `regex` package for the Unicode properties of ECMA-262 patterns.

- **Results and annotations.** Evaluation with a results collector at the Basic, Detailed or Verbose level gives the same rows as the C# `JsonSchemaResultsCollector`, and annotations as `JsonSchemaAnnotationProducer` extracts them.

- **Standalone modules.** A schema can be emitted as a Python module that depends only on the package's small runtime. The `corvus-json-schema` command line generates modules and validates instances.
